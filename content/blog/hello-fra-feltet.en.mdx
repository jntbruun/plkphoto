It always starts with sound.

Not a picture. Not light. A soundtrack you didn't know you were looking for — a wing cutting through still air, a low murmur from grass clumps you can't quite see.

Nature photography is largely the art of waiting. You sit. You stay. You let your body become part of the landscape until birds and animals stop registering you as a threat.

## What it actually requires

People think it's about good equipment. That helps. But the most important skill is the ability to sit still long enough. Longer than is comfortable. Longer than seems rational.

A winter morning at a bird feeding station can mean three hours in minus ten degrees for six seconds that matter.

It's a strange trade. And yet — I wouldn't do it any other way.

## On being patient with the light

Light is in charge. Always. You can have the perfect animal at the perfect moment, but if the light is wrong, the picture is wrong.

Golden hour morning and evening. That's when it happens. When the sun is low and the light is soft and everything looks warmer than it actually is.

I spend a lot of time waiting for that light. And I know that the images I'm most satisfied with — almost all of them came from those hours.
